DEMONSTRATION OF SECTIONS SHOWING TBYPANOSOMA GAMBIENSE IN THE BRAIN SUBSTANCE OF A CASE OF

SLEEPING SICKNESS.

BY A. C. STEVENSON, M.B., M.R.C.8.

The illustrations shown are from the specimens exhibited, stud illustrate the invasion of the extravascular brain tissue by trypanosomes. The sections were from the brain of a case of chronic sleeping sickness which had been under the care of Dr. P. H. MANSON-BAHR, to whose courtesy the opportunity of examining the material was due. The lmrts of the brain examined were the frontal lobe, the parietal lobe, the cerebellum, the pens, and the medulla oblongata. Trypanosomes were found most easily in the frontal lobe, and were also ~en in the pens and medulla oblongats. More extensive examination over larger areas would be necessary to demonstrate their complete distribution, as even in the small areas looked at there appeared to be a tendency to grouping. The trypanosomes were rather more frequent in the white matter than in the grey. The sections were stained with the new modification of the Romanowsky method, viz., "Argovan," followed by very dilute eosin.
