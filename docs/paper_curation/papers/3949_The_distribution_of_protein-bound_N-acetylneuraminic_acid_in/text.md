# The Distribution of Protein-Bound N-Acetylneuraminic Acid in Subcellular Fractions of Rat Brain

By ERIC G. BRUNNGRABER, H. DEKIRMENJIAN AND BARBARA D. BROWN Illinoi8 State P8ychiatric Institute, Chicago, Ia. 60612, U.S.A.

(Received 1 August 1966)

Protein-bound N-acetylneuraminic acid and hexosamine, including the sialomucopolysaccharides, occur mainly in the least dense particles sedimented in the microsomal fraction from rat whole brain. Particles rich in protein-bound N-acetylneuraminic acid and hexosamine are also found in the subcellular fraction separated as a layer between 0-8M- and 1-2M-sucrose after centrifuging the crude mitochondrial preparation in a density gradient. This distribution is similar to that of the gangliosides and suggests an association of all of these substances in thesame subcellularstructures. It ispostulatedthatthesialomucopolysaccharides, as well as the gangliosides, are components of cell membranes. Evidence is presented that indicates that there are quantitative differences between distribution of the gangliosides on the one hand, and protein-bound N-acetylneuraminic acid and hexosamine on the other. The ratio ofprotein-bound N-acetylneuraminic acid (and hexosamine) to gangliosidic N-acetylneuraminic acid (and hexosamine) present in individual subcellular fractions obtained by density-gradient centrifugation tends to increase with increasing particle density. Exposure ofthe crude mitochondrial fraction to osmotic 'shock' before density-gradient centrifugation causes a shift of the protein-bound N-acetylneuraminic acid and gangliosides to the less dense fractions. In some experiments, a selective shift oftheprotein-bound N-acetylneuraminic acid was observed.

The present work is a continuation of investigations designed to ascertain the suboellular localization of the insoluble glycoproteins associated with particulate fractions obtained from rat whole brain. Brunngraber & Brown (1964, 1967) reported that most of the protein-bound NANA* of the isolated particulate matter of rat brain after the gangliosides are extracted is present in a proteinlinked sialomucopolysaccharide. The sialomucopolysaccharides contain NANA, hexose, hexosamine and fucose; the sialomucopolysaccharide molecule consists of a repeating unit containing hexosamine and hexose to which NANA and fucose are attached. Individual sialomucopolysaccharide molecules differ in the relative proportions offucose and NANA attached to this repeating unit. Byproducts obtained in the isolation ofthe sialomucopolysaccharides includeafractionthatwasresistant to a 24hr. incubation period in the presence of papain ('papain-resistant' fraction), as well as diffusible fragments containing some NANA and much hexosamine liberated by the proteolytic procedure.

To draw appropriate conclusions withregardto theirsubcellularlocalization, it was necessary to prepare subcellular fractions of a higher degree of purity than those previously studied. RESULTS

NANA- and hexosamine-containing constituents of subcellular fractions from rat whole brain. The gangliosides were recovered mainly in the fraction of intermediate density (P2B) derived from the crude mitochondrial pellet (Tables 1 and 2). This fraction was also enriched in gangliosides when their concentration was expressed in terms of ,ug. of gangliosidic NANA/mg. of protein. The microsomal pellet (P3) was also rich in gangliosides. These findings agree with those reported by Wolfe (1961). Gangliosides present in the microsomal pellet are especially enriched in the least dense fraction (P3A) derived from it.

The layers were put into centrifuge tubes and made up to 13ml. by filling the tube with water and stirring gently. These were then centrifuged in the no. 40 rotor for 45min. at 105400g. The process was repeated once. The purpose ofthese washings was to remove most ofthe sucrose, which would interfere with the analytical methods. The pellets corresponding to the six layers were then frozen and stored.

The procedure was repeated with three rats at a time, until material from 15 rat brains had been accumulated. The corresponding fractions obtained from each experiment were combined to give a 15 rat preparation of layers P3A, P3B, P30, P2A, P2B and P2C.

The distribution ofthesialomucopolysaccharides, diffusible fragments and papain-resistant material is similar to that of the gangliosides in all these respects. It appears that all of the hexosamineand NANA-containing substances are closely associated in brain tissue and may be present in the same subcellular structures. Fraction P2B is known to be enriched in nerve endings and membranes (Whittaker, 1959; Gray & Whittaker, 1962); fraction P3A is reported to contain membranous fragments (Toschi, 1959).

A crude mitochondrial pellet from cat cerebellum was prepared and subfractionated into five fractions (P2A', P2B', P20', P2D' and P2E') by the procedure described by De Robertis, Pellegrino de Iraldi, Rodriguez de Lores Arnaiz & Salganicoff (1962).

Osmotic-'shock' treatment of the crude mitochondrial pellet from whole rat brain consisted of successively suspending the mitochondrial pellet obtained by procedures described by Brunngraber & Brown (1964) in 0-02M-trischloride buffer, pH8-0, centrifuging at 105000g for 30min., resuspending in water and recentrifuging. Suspension of the particulate fraction was performed by homogenizing by hand in a TenBroeck homogenizer. This procedure solubilizes approx. 25% of the protein content of the pellet (Brunngraber & Aguilar, 1962), and additional treatment solubilizes very little more. The 'shocked' material was separated on a density gradient consisting ofequal volumes of 0-8M- and 1-2M-sucrose to give three fractions PMA, P2BM and P2C" analogous to fractions P2A, P2B and P2C.

The major NANA-containing materials in rat brain are the gangliosides, the 'papain-resistant' fraction, and the sialomucopolysaccharides. Diffusible NANA-containing fragments released by the action of papain represent only about 4-10% of the total brain content of NANA, and of the NANA content of subcellular fractions prepared from brain. The 'papain-resistant' material contains largely sialomucopolysaccharides that have not been removed by papain by the 24hr. digestion procedure, aswellas some gangliosides notremoved by extraction with chloroform-methanol (2: 1, v/v) (Suzuki, 1965).

The defatted protein residue was subjected to proteolytic digestion with papain. The papain-resistant material was removed by centrifugation. The NANA- and hexosamine-containing materials solubilized by papain were dialysed to recover the diffusible fragments. The high-molecular-weight constituents were treated with cetylpyridinium chloride to precipitate the nucleic acids and glucuronic acid-containing mucopolysaccharides. Details ofthis procedure have been published (Brunngraber & Brown, 1964, 1967).

The concentration of these substances, when expressed in terms of ,ug. of NANA or hexosamine/mg. ofprotein, is also higher. The concentration ofall ofthese substances is also greater in the microsomal fraction than it is in the mitochondrial fraction. Moreover, they are predominantlypresent in the less dense particles ofthe crude microsomal pellet. These particles are believed to consist of fragments of nerve-cell membranes (Wherrett & Mcllwain, 1962; Toschi, 1959; Hanzon & Toschi, 1959, 1960).

Abood, Kurahasi, Brunngraber & Koketsu (1966) have isolated sialomucopolysaccharides from frog sartorius-muscle membrane preparations by procedures identical with the ones used here. In the above paper, it was erroneously reported that the sialomucopolysaccharides of the preparation are precipitated by cetylpyridinium chloride. The sialomucopolysaccharides from muscle membrane are in fact similar to those ofrat brain in that they are not precipitated by this reagent.

The cell membrane of the Ehrlich ascitescarcinoma cell (Cook, Laico & Eylar, 1965) and the red blood cell (Cook & Eylar, 1965) also appear to contain non-ganglioside, membrane-bound NANA and hexosamine; thus the protein-bound and hexosamine-containing constituents may have a membrane function as well as the gangliosides (McIlwain, 1963).

The present work suggests that the mitochondria (P2C), RNA granules (P3C) and lysosomes (P2C) do not contain appreciable amounts of NANA or hexosamine. The lysosomes have been demonstrated to be present in the most dense fractions of the crude mitochondrial preparation (Koenig, Gaines, McDonald, Gray & Scott, 1964). The nuclear pellet is relatively poor in these substances (Brunngraber & Brown, 1964), and recent experiments in this Laboratory have indicated that most of the NANA and hexosamine that appear in this preparation are present in the less dense fractions and not in the more dense, nuclei-enriched, fractions. The myelin fraction (P2A) contains appreciable amounts of hexosamine and NANA, but purified myelin from ox-brain white matter (Autilio, Norton & Terry, 1964) contains less than 5% of proteins that are insoluble in chloroformmethanol (2: 1, v/v).

The present work has provided evidence that the protein-bound NANA/gangliosidic NANA ratio increases with particle density. This is not entirely unexpected, since it is known that this ratio varies considerably in different parts of the brain (Papadopoulos, 1960). There is no reason to believe that the entire neuronal membrane is of the same composition. One might expect chemical differences in membranes that are involved in the processes associated with the inhibitory postsynaptic potential, excitatory post-synaptic potential and the action potential (Eccles, 1964). The physiological attributes of differing regions of the nerve-cell membrane, such as the axon, axon hillock, perikaryon, pre-synaptic and post-synaptic membranes, suggest differences in chemical structure. Further differences may be associated with the glial membranes, which must be present in the suboellular particulates obtained after homogenizing nerve tissue.

Hess (1953) reported a positive periodic acidSchiff reaction at the periphery of nerve cells that was attributed to the presence of mucopolysaccharides in the intercellular cement substance. In view of the low volume of extracellular space in brain, this positive reaction may be a reflection of the nerve-cell membrane itself.
