- (a) Foranaerobic experiment8. Exhaustive washing of the tissue was necessary in order to produce a very low residual activity. The method of preparation [cf. Long & Peters, 1939] was as follows:

Lipmann [1937] has shown that inorganic phosphate or arsenate is necessary for 02 uptake with acetone-dried preparations of B. delbriikii in the presence ofpyruvate and methylene blue. The relation of 02 absorbed and CO2 produced to pyruvate utilized, indicated oxidative decarboxylation to acetic acid. Banga et al. [1939b] observed that phosphate markedly increased the 02 uptake of dialysed pigeon brain suspensions ijn presence of pyruvate, adenine nucleotide, fumarate and Mg++. They inferred that the respiration would be zero in the complete absence of inorganic phosphate. To simplify the problem, the experiments described in this section were carried out in order to test the effect ofinorganic phosphate, only, on the oxidation of pyruvate and a-ketobutyrate by pigeon brain suspensions, in the absence of other factors.

The cerebrum and optic lobes (about 1-5 g.) were removed as rapidly as possible from a freshly killed pigeon, and ground, ice-cold, in a mortar for 10 min. 0 9% KCI (6 ml.), ice-cold, was then added in portions and the grinding continued for a further 5 min. After passing through muslin, the brain suspension was centrifuged and the supernatant fluid discarded. The deposit was washed twice with 0.9% KC1 and then three times with 0-2% KCI (6 ml. each time), keeping throughout as cold as possible and allowing the salt solutions to remain in contact with the enzyme for about 15 min. before each centrifugation. The washed, almost colourless, suspension, now free from cytochromes, was made up to 10 ml. with 0-2% KCI. 1 ml. suspension was equivalent to 150 mg. fresh brain tissue.

(b) For aerobic experiment8. The preparation of dialysed brain suspensions, except in the temperature during extraction, resembled that of the 'dispersions' described by Banga et al. [1939b].

While much information is available on the biological value of isolated proteins and of mixed proteins of individual foodstuffs, relatively little is known about the biological value of the proteins (nitrogen) of mixed human dietaries.
