The authors of this paper discovered a definite sign in three cases, which they describe at length, which they are convinced is unequivocal evidence of a frontal tumour. They have collected ten further cases from the literature.

This special sign was found in the upper limb of the side opposite to the diseased frontal lobe and consists of the presence of involuntary grasping and groping movements. These are elicited by stroking gently

across the palmer surface of the finger tips when the fingers make a slow grasping movement most obvious in the thumb and index finger. The movement is a combination of flexion and extension. If an object be placed in the open palm the hand closes firmly on it, and on attempting to gently withdraw it, the grasp tightens promptly and increases in power as the observer uses more and more strength to overcome it, until the grasp is so firm that the patient may be lifted from the pillow and dragged across the bed. The movements occur without the patient's knowledge, and even when his attention is drawn to them he is unable to inhibit them. No difficulty may be experienced in quickly opening and closing the empty hand. It occurs where there are no psychical disorders, where sensation is intact, and before any weakness or altered tone can be detected. It is not a manifestation of apraxia. The memory is unimpaired. The will to open the hand is there, the extensors are innervated

powerfully but the flexors fail to relax.

The clue to this symptom is found in a study of grasping in the infant. The new-born infant shows reflex and persistent graspingLater it begins to grope and reach for objects, but only closes the hand in response to contact. Later still it learns to pick up objects but is unable to relax the hand; finally both opening and closing movements are performed with

ease. The earlier automatic and immediate reactions of early infancy come later under the control of a mechanism on a higher physiological level. When this control is removed by injury or disease, a reappearance of reactions analogous with those of an earlier type of development may be expected.
