Antibodies to N-CAM specifically inhibit the aggregation ofcellsfrom nerve tissue (3), perturb the normal patterns of histotypic differentiation in cultured cell aggregates (6) and retinal tissue (7), inhibit the fasciculation of neurites (8), alter the response of ganglionic processes to nerve growth factor (9), and prevent the binding of nerve cells to muscle cells in vitro (10). ABSTRACT Chemical analyses andbindingstudieshave been correlatedtoclarifythe relationshipofstructuretofunction inthe neural cell adhesion molecule (N-CAM) from embryonic chicken brain. N-CAM isolated from the cell surface appears to include two closely related polypeptide chains. Treatment with neuraminidase of such preparations of N-CAM bound by antibodies on solid supports yielded components of Mr 140,000 and 170,000. These components each had the same amino-terminal sequence as N-CAM and gave nearly identical profiles on peptide maps. Immunoprecipitation of N-CAM from 9-day brain cells treated with tunicamycin yielded corresponding components of Mr 130,000 and 160,000, suggesting that the differences between these two componentsof N-CAM areinthepolypeptide ratherthanthe carbohydrate portions of the molecules. N-CAM appears to be oriented with the amino terminus extending away from the cell surface and with the bulk of the sialic acid near the middle of the peptidechain. As shownpreviously, incubationofN-CAMat370C generates a fragment (FrI) of Mr 65,000 that lacks most of the sialic acid. Treatment of membranes with Staphylococcus aureus V-8 protease released a fragment (Fr2) of N-CAM that contained most of the sialic acid; this fragment had an Mr of 108,000 after neuraminidase treatment. Both of these fragments contain the amino-terminal portion of the polypeptide chain. At least a portionofthe N-CAMbindingsitewasfoundtobelocatedintheaminoterminal region of the peptide chain. Most or all of the sialic acid was not directly involved in binding, although it can influence binding, as indicated by the finding that neuraminidase-treated N-CAM (desialylated-N-CAM) bound to cells to a greater extent than untreated N-CAM. The FrI and the Fr2 fragments in solution did not bind to cells but were as effective as N-CAM and desialylated-N-CAM as competitors for N-CAM binding to cells. When fixed covalently to beads, N-CAM, desialylated-N-CAM, and the Fri and Fr2 fragments bound specifically to cells. In contrast, the N-CAM autolysis products released along with Frl neitherbound tocells norcompeted for N-CAM binding. In addition to suggesting a location for the N-CAM binding region, the accumulated results raise the possibility that valence may play akey role in N-CAM binding.

Methods for obtaining chicken N-CAM in milligram quantities have facilitated the analysis of the chemical composition and overall properties of the molecule (4). N-CAM is a glycoprotein with an unusually large amount (30% by weight) of sialic acid, is polydisperse in solution, and is associated with proteolytic activity leading to its spontaneous cleavage. There is evidence that N-CAM plays a direct role as a ligand in cellcell binding (15)-i.e., N-CAM binding is second-order homophilic (1). The present study describes an analysis of the NCAM molecule in terms ofthe organization of certain ofits key structural features and their contribution to N-CAM binding.

Release of N-CAM from Membranes. To determine the properties and orientation of N-CAM on the cell surface, we tested avariety of proteases for their ability to release the molecule from membranes. A large sialic acid-rich fragment of NCAM was released in soluble form by limitedproteolysis ofbrain vesicles with S. aureus V-8 protease. Up to one-third of the NCAM was released by this treatment, whereas the remainder appeared relatively undegraded and stayed on the vesicles. Immunoaffinity chromatography (4) of the released material gave a variety of components (Fig. LA, lane 6), the largest of which moved as abroad zone of Mr 140,000-200,000 on NaDodSO4/ polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis. This component (Fig. 1A, lane 7) could be further purified by gel filtration. The molar ratio of sialic acid to total amino acids was 0.22 as compared to 0.13 for intact N-CAM, suggesting that this species contains greater than 80% of the sialic acid present in intact N-CAM. We designate this fragment Fr2.

These polypeptides are apparently not expressed on the cell surface. Unlike N-CAM freshly synthesized in the absence of tunicamycin, they are not degraded when brain cells are treated with trypsin, although tunicamycin-treated cells doretain some unlabeled N-CAM on their surface thatis degraded by trypsin. In addition, trypsin-treated retinal cells that were allowed to recover in the presence of tunicamycin synthesized the two polypeptides, but, unlikecontrolcells, theyproducednoN-CAM on their surfaces detectable with "2I-labeled monoclonal antiN-CAM.

Treatment of Fr2 with neuraminidase gave a major component of Mr 108,000 (Fig. 1A, lane 8) on NaDodSO4/polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis. DISCUSSION

Earlier studies from this laboratory indicated that various interactions between nerve cells are mediated by the neural cell adhesion molecule, N-CAM, and suggested (1, 15) that the binding was homophilic (i.e., N-CAM to N-CAM). In conclusion, these results indicate that the main region of N-CAM involved in formation of a bond between cells is localized in the amino-terminal region of the molecule representedbythe Frl fragment (Fig. 4). The bindingof monomeric N-CAM to cells is apparently weak, so that significant binding occurs only between surfaces containing a number of N-CAM molecules. A cell membrane is such a surface. Furthermore, the possible alterations of binding by changes in valence could beimportantin the makingandbreakingofcell-cellbonds during development.

The apparent sensitivity of N-CAM to proteolysis and the presence of weak proteolytic activity in preparations of N-CAM raises the question of whether such modifications could modulate N-CAM function, most obviously by breaking cell-cell bonds (1, 4). A more detailed description of N-CAM function will be required to allow evaluation of this possibility.

The results on binding presented here suggest that neuraminidase-treated N-CAM binds more effectively than untreated N-CAM. As suggested by the. local cell surface modulation hypothesis for neuron-neuron binding (1), the strength of cell-cell binding may be controlled by changes in the sialic acid-content of N-CAM. The fact that the sialic acid content of N-CAM varies in different tissues (4, 14), as well as with age (5, 14), is consistent with this hypothesis.
