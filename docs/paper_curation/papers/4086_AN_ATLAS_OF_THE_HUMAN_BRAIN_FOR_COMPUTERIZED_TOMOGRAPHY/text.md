This conceptualization of depression as stemming from disordered thought processes facilitates the view that antipsychotic agents may be the drugs of choice for therapy.

Anatomic sections are illustrated by means of several other tube angulations related to the baseline. These will be useful in the interpretation of scans when the patient cannot be positioned for routine scans or when coronal sections are taken.

Does the author really mean that depressive delusions initiate depression and that major tranquillizers are indicated rather than antidepressant drugs? It appears that he does, for he cites a case illustrating this, though one could argue equally well that this particular patient was not treated adequately with antidepressants or that she was suffering from a schizoaffective psychosis that required treatment of both components.

The authors have formulated a "level index" to obtain equivalent CT scan levels in patients with different brain sizes: a simple formula is used to get the correct level to scan for any section of the brain.

In general the writing style is direct and concise; hence, the author was able to include the essential material on the topic. It is a useful reference work for practising physicians, although its greatest value is probably as a textbook for senior undergraduate and graduate students of work physiology. Regrettably, the high cost may restrict its appeal.

The illustrations and the photographs of brain sections are large and detailed. Unfortunately the detail on the CT scans is not as good as would have been obtained with the newer scanners.

Lion's last chapter is much clearer and therefore much more useful. In it he discusses questions raised by patients, such as: "Do I have to take this medication?" "What are some side effects?" "The medication makes me feel bad." "Will I become addicted?" "Can I have a drink with it?" "Can I drive?" These and other valid points are dealt with adequately in the last 16 pages of the book.

This atlas will be particularly useful to neurosurgeons and to radiologists interpreting CT scans since it promotes more accurate localization of lesions in the axial plane. Although coronal and sagittal reconstruction methods in CT scanning will make it easier to locate lesions, they are not routinely available yet.
