Brain Research from 1900 to 2026: A Metadata-Driven Review

Generated: 2026-07-01 · Dataset: 12,700 papers

Abstract

This draft review maps research on the relationship between brain structure and AI from 1900 through 2026, re-scoring up to 1,000 public Semantic Scholar candidate papers per year for structural brain signals and AI/modeling relevance before selecting 100 papers per year by citation count. The final 12,700 selected papers are organized into neuroimaging AI, connectomics and graph models, decoding and BCI, computational neuroscience and brain-inspired AI, cellular and neuromorphic foundations, cognitive architectures, clinical NeuroAI, electrophysiology decoding, and broad reviews.

Key Findings

  • The selected papers account for 4,897,708 citations in the selected set, with the largest citation mass in 2009.
  • The largest taxonomy category is General Brain-AI Structure Reviews and Methods (4,034 papers).
  • Keyword filters expose method and population axes such as MRI, fMRI, EEG, MEG, ECoG, single-cell, human, and non-human.
  • The result is a metadata-driven map rather than a PDF-level systematic review, so expert appraisal remains necessary before strong field-level claims.

Future Research Agenda

  • Add full-text appraisal for study design, sample size, reproducibility, data availability, and code release.
  • Extend the taxonomy with clinical translation, long-term stability, safety, neuroethics, and privacy criteria.
  • Use expert review or recency-aware scoring to compensate for structurally low citation counts in recent years.
