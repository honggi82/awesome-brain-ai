In an analysis of about 1,000 cases made in the Roentgen department of the Children's Hospital in Boston, the late Dr. Thomas M. Dr. Rotch found that the normal changes which take place in the bones of the wrist correspond so closely to those of the other joints that, in the great majority of individuals, the wrist may be accepted as a fairly correct index of general develop¬ ment ; he also found that the normal process of ossifica¬ tion, as shown by the wrists, was frequently retarded and, in connection with such cases, he called attention to the danger of strain ; but, so far as I know, gave no specific illustrations.
