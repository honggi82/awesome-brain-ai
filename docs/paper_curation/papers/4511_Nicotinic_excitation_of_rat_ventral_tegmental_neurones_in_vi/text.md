### Nicotinic excitation of rat ventral tegmental neurones in vitro studied by intracellular recording

##### 'P. Calabresi, 2M.G. Lacey & 3R.A. North

Vollum Institute, Oregon Health Sciences University, Portland, Oregon 97201, U.S.A.

- 1 Intracellular recordings were made from presumed dopamine-containing neurones in the ventral tegmental area (VTA) in rat brain slices.
- 2 Nicotine (10-100 uM) and acetylcholine (ACh) depolarized the neurones. The depolarization caused by ACh was typically biphasic; both components were increased by neostigmine (0.1-10pM), but only the slower component was blocked by scopolamine (1-10 pM).
- 3 The nicotinic action of ACh, studied in the presence of neostigmine and scopolamine, persisted in the presence of tetrodotoxin (1 pM) and cobalt (2-5 mM).
- 4 ACh or carbachol (30pM) caused inward currents in neurones voltage-clamped near the resting potential. These currents reversed polarity at around -4mV, were blocked by hexamethonium (1-100pM) in a voltage-dependent manner, and showed desensitization with prolonged or repeated agonist applications.
- 5 Depolarizations caused by ACh and carbachol were reduced in slices pretreated with Kbungarotoxin, but were not changed by a-bungarotoxin.
- 6 These responses to ACh and nicotine resemble those previously described on autonomic ganglion cells. The direct action on VTA neurones may contribute to the positive reinforcement associated with nicotine consumption.

Nicotine, applied systemically or by iontophoresis, excites neurones in both the substantia nigra (Lichtensteiger et al., 1982; Clarke et al., 1985) and ventral tegmental area (VTA) (Grenhoff et al., 1986; Mereu et al., 1987) of rats. Nicotine also increases dopamine concentrations in terminal regions of these cells, as do cocaine, amphetamine and morphine (DiChiara & Imperato, 1988). These findings are consistent with the idea that the excitation of VTA neurones by nicotine contributes to the reinforcement associated with tobacco

Although autoradiographic studies suggest that [3H]-nicotine binding sites are on dopaminecontaining neurones themselves (Clarke & Pert, 1985), electrophysiological experiments using extracellular recording in vivo are generally not able to resolve whether the nicotinic excitation is taking place on these cells or on other neurones afferent to them. Nor are those methods well suited to determine the type of nicotinic receptor involved. The purpose of the present experiments was to examine the actions of ACh and nicotine, by using intracellular recording from rat VTA neurones in vitro.

###### Effects ofnicotine and acetylcholine

With the exception of ACh, drugs were applied by changing the superfusion solution to one containing known concentrations of drug; new solutions entered the recording chamber within 30s. The drugs used were: nicotine sulphate, carbamylcholine chloride (carbachol), (-)-scopolamine hydrochloride, hexamethonium chloride (Sigma), tetrodotoxin (TTX) and neostigmine methylsulphate (Calbiochem). ACh (Sigma) was applied by ejecting a few nl of a 100mm solution from the tip of a blunt pipette positioned beneath the surface of the superfusing solution and just above the tissue slice; a pressure of 50-70kPa for 50-500ms was typically used.

Fifty-nine cells were studied; all had properties similar to those of principal dopamine-containing neurones in the adjacent substantia nigra zona compacta, including hyperpolarization by dopamine (Lacey et al., 1987; 1989). Nicotine (10-100Mm) depolarized the neurones and increased their firing rate, or caused an inward current in cells held at the resting potential (n = 5) (Figures la and 2c).

Block by hexamethonium is typical of nicotinic actions in ganglia (Paton & Zaimis, 1949) and brain (Egan & North, 1986; McCormick & Prince, 1987): its voltage-dependence (Figure 3b) has previously been shown in ganglion cells (Rang, 1982). The finding of voltage-dependent block by hexamethonium, along with the insensitivity of the response to TTX and cobalt, indicate that the response was due to direct activation of nicotinic receptors on VTA neurones. The cross-desensitization with nicotine and carbachol (Figure 2b and c) suggests that all three agents act upon the same receptors on these cells.

K-Bungarotoxin blocks nicotinic receptors on ganglion cells, but not at the neuromuscular junction, in contradistinction to a-bungarotoxin (Chiappinelli, 1983; Loring et al., 1985; Loring & Zigmond, 1988). It also blocks the nicotinic receptor expressed in the membrane of oocytes injected with mRNA directing the synthesis of o3 and a4 agonist-binding nicotinic receptor subunits (Boulter et al., 1987), but not a2 (Wada et al., 1988). In situ hybridization experiments have demonstrated the presence of mRNA encoding the synthesis of a3 and c4 (but not a2: Wada et al., 1988) subunits in the VTA (Goldman et al., 1986;

- 1987). Thus it may be that the nicotinic depolarization is mediated by receptors comprising either %3 or a4 subunits (or both). However, the failure to block completely the response with K-bungarotoxin must necessarily temper this conclusion. It is possible that a mixed population of K-bungarotoxin-sensitive and -insensitive receptors is present, or that in our experiments the binding of K-bungarotoxin may have been incomplete at the time during which recordings were made.
