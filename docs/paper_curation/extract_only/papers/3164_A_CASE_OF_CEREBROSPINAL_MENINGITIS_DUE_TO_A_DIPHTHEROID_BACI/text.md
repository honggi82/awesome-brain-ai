The bacillus grew both aerobically and anaerobically on the surface of goat blood agar, producing a delicate grayish film, or isolated, slightly convex, grayish colonies less than a millimeter in diameter. The blood was not changed. The growth on goat blood agar was more luxuriant than on plain agar or Loeffler's serum. The bacillus grew but produced no acid or gas in maltose, raffinose, saccharose, lactose, salicin, mannite, dulcite, inulin, galactose, or dextrin azolitmin broth. In glucose azolitmin broth, it produced acid without gas. It had no effect on milk. Broth cultures were clear, with a finely granular sediment. In twenty-four hour old cultures the bacillus was not motile.

A CASE OF CEREBROSPINAL MENINGITIS DUE TO A DIPHTHEROID

A guinea-pig inoculated intravenously with two twenty-four hour slants of the original culture died twenty-two hours later. There was a marked fatty degeneration of the liver, and a blood-tinged cerebrospinal fluid, which contained the bacillus in pure culture. Two guinea-pigs were inoculated intraperitoneally with a forty-eight hour broth culture of the organism, and one of them also received diphtheria antitoxin ; neither of these pigs died.
